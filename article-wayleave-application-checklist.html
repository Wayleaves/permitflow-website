Wayleave Application Checklist: Documents Required for Infrastructure Projects in South Africa

A practical reference covering the core documents required for SANRAL, municipal, Eskom, Transnet, and private landowner wayleave applications — bookmark this before your next submission.

Back to all articles

Incomplete applications are the single biggest cause of avoidable delays in South African infrastructure permitting. Every authority has its own requirements — and submitting to SANRAL with a municipal-format package, or missing a cross-section drawing from an Eskom submission, means a return and a restart.

This checklist covers the core documents required for each major authority type. Use it before every submission.

Important: Requirements vary by authority, region, and project complexity. This checklist covers the standard requirements — always verify the specific requirements of the authority you're submitting to before finalising your package.

Documents Required for All Applications

These documents are required regardless of which authority you're applying to:

Universal Requirements

All authorities
Formal application letterAddressed to the correct authority and department, identifying the applicant, the infrastructure, and the location.
Must be on company letterhead and signed by an authorised representative.
Locality planA map showing the crossing location in its wider geographic context — province, town, road name, and route.
Google Maps screenshot is acceptable for locality only — not for the detailed engineering drawings.
GPS coordinates of the crossingIn decimal degrees format. Must match the location shown on the engineering drawings.
Incorrect coordinates are a common rejection trigger — verify against the drawings before submitting.
Company registration documentsCIPC certificate of incorporation and, where applicable, proof of VAT registration.
Motivation letter or technical justificationExplains the purpose of the infrastructure, the public benefit, and why the chosen crossing point was selected.

SANRAL Wayleave Checklist

SANRAL — National Road Crossings

N-routes
Engineering drawings — plan viewShowing road reserve boundary, carriageway, and proposed infrastructure route. Must be to scale.
Must be signed and stamped by a registered professional engineer or technologist (ECSA registered).
Engineering drawings — cross-sectionShowing depth of burial, casing or sleeve specifications, clearance from road surface and layer works.
This is the drawing most commonly missing from incomplete applications.
Method statementDetailed construction methodology — equipment, trenchless technique specifications, staging areas, timeline, reinstatement plan.
"Directional drilling" alone is not sufficient. Specify equipment, dimensions, and sequence.
Traffic Accommodation Plan (TAP)Required for any works that may affect traffic on or adjacent to the national road. Must comply with SARTSM Volume 3.
Proof of engineer's ECSA registrationConfirmation that the engineer who prepared and stamped the drawings is currently registered with the Engineering Council of South Africa.
Correct regional office identifiedNorthern, Southern, Eastern, or Western — determined by the location of the crossing.
Submitting to the wrong region adds weeks while the application is transferred internally.

Municipal Wayleave Checklist

Municipal — Metro and Local Municipalities

All municipalities
Engineering drawingsLayout plan showing the road reserve, carriageway, footpaths, and infrastructure route. Cross-sections where the authority requires them.
Some metros (Joburg, Cape Town) have specific drawing standards. Check before preparing.
Authority-specific application formMany municipalities have their own application forms — check the relevant municipal website or engineering department before submitting a letter-only application.
Traffic accommodation planRequired where works affect traffic flow. Some municipalities accept a simplified plan for minor works.
Reinstatement specificationDescription of how the road surface, pavement, and verge will be reinstated after installation — to the municipality's specification.
Correct department identifiedRoads, infrastructure, or electrical departments handle different applications. Submitting to the wrong internal department delays processing.
In smaller municipalities, direct engagement to identify the right person before submitting is often worth the time.

Eskom Wayleave Checklist

Eskom — Power Line Servitude Crossings

Transmission & Distribution
Correct Eskom division identifiedTransmission (66kV and above) and Distribution (below 66kV) handle applications separately. Confirm the voltage of the line you're crossing before submitting.
Engineering drawings showing clearancesMust show the clearance between your infrastructure and the power line, in compliance with Eskom's technical standards and SANS 10280.
Eskom servitude detailsThe registered servitude number and width, obtained from the Deeds Office or Eskom directly. Applications without this are commonly returned.
Electromagnetic compatibility assessmentFor fibre and telecoms crossings under high-voltage lines, a basic EMC assessment showing the installation won't be adversely affected by the electromagnetic field.
Casing and protection specificationsWhere your infrastructure crosses under Eskom's servitude, casing or conduit specifications showing the protective measures in place.

Private Landowner Checklist

Private Landowner Access Agreement

Private land
Title deed confirmationConfirm the registered owner of the land before approaching anyone. Who you think owns the property and who legally owns it are not always the same.
Infrastructure description and route planA clear, simple explanation of what is being installed, where, and at what depth — in language a non-technical landowner can understand.
Written access agreementA formal agreement covering the permitted activities, duration, compensation, reinstatement obligations, and dispute resolution. Under the 2026 ICASA regulations, this is now mandatory for ECNS licensees before deployment begins.
Do not rely on verbal agreements — they are unenforceable and create risk for both parties.
Compensation offer documentedGood faith compensation negotiations must be documented. The 2026 framework requires consideration of market value and demonstrable loss.
Proof of consultationUnder the new ICASA regulations, evidence that mandatory consultation with the landowner took place before deployment commenced.
